How I work with engineers

Fewer meetings, not more

Engineers are right to distrust process people who add ceremonies. I was a developer for five years before I led delivery. I start from the data already in your tracker, cut meetings that don't change a decision, and automate the status work instead of asking people for more updates.

A good fit

  • A remote or hybrid software team of roughly 15–60 engineers
  • At least 3 months of history in Jira, Azure DevOps or Asana
  • A leader who owns delivery dates
  • Dates slipping, or status work eating your managers' week

Not a fit

  • Teams of fewer than about 8 engineers
  • You need someone to write code or own the architecture
  • You want individual developer rankings
  • You are hiring for a full-time role

The method

Flow to Forecast

Four steps from opaque delivery to dates you can plan around. Each step ends with something your team keeps.

  1. 1

    Map the real flow

    Short interviews and your tool history show how work actually moves, including the handoffs nobody documented.

    You getA map of the real workflow, with the waiting time at each step

  2. 2

    Measure

    Cycle time, throughput and work in progress, pulled from 3–6 months of your tracker history.

    You getA baseline and a metrics board your team can read

  3. 3

    Fix the constraint

    Remove the biggest bottleneck first: limit work in progress, redesign the worst handoffs, keep only the meetings that change decisions.

    You getMore work finished with the same people

  4. 4

    Automate and forecast

    AI agents take over status relay and reporting. Dates come from real throughput, as ranges with a stated confidence.

    You getA live executive readout and forecasts your team runs without me

The Health Check covers steps 1 and 2 and a first forecast. Steps 3 and 4 are the Fractional Delivery Lead and Status on Autopilot.
