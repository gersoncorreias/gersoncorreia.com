The method

Flow to Forecast: four steps from opaque delivery to dates you can plan around

Each step ends with something your team keeps, and each one pays for itself before the next begins.

  1. 01

    Map the real flow

    Owner-led sessions and the data already in your tools show how work actually moves, including the handoffs nobody documented.

    You get
    A map of the real workflow, request to release
    Value
    You see where work waits, in days, not opinions
  2. 02

    Measure

    Cycle time, throughput and work in progress, pulled from your Jira, Azure DevOps or Asana history.

    You get
    A live flow metrics board and a baseline
    Value
    One shared picture for teams and leadership
  3. 03

    Fix the constraint

    Remove the biggest bottleneck first: limit work in progress, redesign the worst handoffs, set cadences people keep.

    You get
    A 30-day plan, run together with your team
    Value
    More work finished with the same people
  4. 04 AI-assisted

    Automate and forecast

    AI agents take over status relay and reporting. Delivery dates come from real throughput, as ranges with a stated confidence.

    You get
    A live executive readout and delivery forecasts, handed over to your team
    Value
    Leaders plan on forecasts; managers get their week back

Cloud ERP software company · 4 product squads

From capacity debates to flow data

Challenge
Four squads with no shared view of flow. Capacity was argued by opinion, and a core financial module had to ship without disrupting customers.
Approach
A Lean metrics board (cycle time, lead time, throughput), squads trained to act on it, and an MVP-first rollout split into parallel workstreams.
Impact
25% more delivery capacity without hiring. The financial module went live without disrupting users.
25+strategic projects steered in one portfolio

Medical education group · directors and a PMO team

A portfolio leadership could actually steer

Challenge
More than 25 projects competing for approval and capacity. Business cases were often sent back, and directors lacked a current view.
Approach
A maturity scorecard for business cases, an approval process with pre-alignment and sign-off gates, and live portfolio dashboards.
Impact
Less rework at approval, a shorter approval cycle, and real-time visibility for directors.

Status reporting that rebuilds itself

Challenge
The real state of work lived in Slack threads; the tracked state lived in Asana. Someone retyped one into the other every cycle.
Approach
Mapped the cross-team lifecycle with directors and leads, then built an AI agent that moves Slack updates onto the right task.
Impact
Reporting became a rebuild instead of a rewrite, and the gaps between documented and real process were made visible.
